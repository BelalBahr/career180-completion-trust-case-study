Summary

  • Problem: a certificate shows completion, not evidence, so a partner cannot tell what it means.
  • Decision: keep completion rules as they are, and show an evidence level on a public certificate check page: L0 Participation, L1 Assessed, L2 Verified.
  • Old certificates: keep them, label them Legacy, set the level from records we already hold, and tell learners before launch.
  • Who pays: Growth keeps "certified learners" and adds "assessed learners" beside it. Content loses nothing on completion. The platform funds a small payout kicker and part-time trust ops. Partners accept that about 55% of certificates read "not assessed".
  • MVP: 8 items, 2 backend and 1 frontend engineer, 13 weeks. The learner upgrade flow is a stretch item.
  • Biggest risks: quiz data quality, Legal lead time, and quizzes getting easier once L1 matters.

PART 1

Problem & Solution Brief

The decision memo: what a certificate should mean, what we do about old certificates, who pays, and what ships in one quarter.

1.1Problem framing and labelling convention

Completion is cheap to fake (skip ahead, leave a tab open, click through a quiz), and partners have started asking what a certificate actually proves. The root problem is not that certificates are too easy to earn. It is that the certificate carries no evidence a partner can inspect, so every certificate looks equally trustworthy or equally worthless.

Every key claim in this document is labelled: Brief backed by the case brief, Assumption my assumption, with a way to check it.

Terms used in this document

  • Certified learner: a learner who completed a course and received a certificate. It means they are learning. This meaning does not change.
  • Assessed learner: a certified learner whose certificate has assessment evidence (level L1 or L2). It is a separate, additional claim about skills.
  • Certificate check: the public page where a partner enters an ID and sees the evidence. The word "Verified" is reserved for the L2 level only.
Position in one sentence
A certificate should state what evidence stands behind it. "Certified" keeps meaning "completed and learning"; whether the learner gained the skill is a separate claim that we show as an assessment level. Completion rate is untouched, so instructors and Growth are not forced to fight the change.

1.2Trust model

Three evidence levels. The partner sees a level and the evidence behind it, not a pass/fail flag.

LevelEvidence requiredWhat the partner is toldGood for
L0 ParticipationCourse completed, no assessment"Completed the course. Competence was not assessed."Showing the learner took part
L1 AssessedAuto-graded quiz passed, with integrity checks: minimum time spent, at most 3 attempts per 72 hours"Passed an assessment. Identity not verified."A screening signal
L2 VerifiedProctored or project-based exam passed, with identity check"Passed a proctored or reviewed assessment, with identity confirmed."The strongest evidence we offer

Rules

  • Evidence only moves a level up. A level moves down only through a review outcome. Engagement signals (watch-time plausibility, single-session bursts) can attach a flag to a record. They never raise the level.
  • Courses with no assessment cap at L0. We say so honestly and do not invent a signal. Course owners can opt up by adding a quiz. Assumption that most of the catalog stays at L0 for now.
  • Corporate learners use the same bar. The record shows the enrollment source (bulk or self-directed) as context, with no penalty. An assessment pass is what separates real effort from a clicked-through course, so it works equally for both. Brief that about a third of learners are corporate and harder to read.
  • A certificate is pinned to the course version it was issued against. If the course was later revised, the record shows both versions and whether the change was material.
  • No expiry in the MVP. The brief notes there is no expiry today Brief. Adding one is a post-MVP decision.
Legal
Every level is defined by documented, checkable criteria, published on a methodology page. The word "Verified" is used only for L2, which is why the public page is called a certificate check. Brief that Legal requires a defensible methodology behind any "verified" claim.

1.3Certificates already issued

Decision: keep them valid, label them Legacy, and backfill a level from evidence we already hold. Never revoke, never upgrade beyond what the evidence supports.

  • Every certificate issued before the cutover date gets Standard = Legacy. The cutover date is a configuration value set at launch.
  • A level is computed from existing quiz and exam records using the same rules. No data means L0 Legacy. Partial data (a quiz pass is recorded but timing or attempt records are missing) also stays L0, because the integrity checks for L1 cannot be applied.
  • The partner sees "Issued under the pre-cutover standard" next to the level, so an old certificate is never presented as equivalent to a new one.
  • Legacy certificates are subject to the same integrity rules. Some will show a lower level than the holder expects, so every affected learner is told before the page goes live and can contest the result (PRD item 6).
  • Revoking would break promises already made to learners and employers. Grandfathering silently would repeat the trust problem. Labelling is the position that is both honest and cheap.

Assumption historical quiz and exam records exist in a usable form for most assessed courses, including timestamps and attempt counts. Check: weeks 1 to 3, sample 50 assessed courses and measure how many quiz passes have complete timing and attempt records. A first backfill dry run follows in week 6, not at the end.

1.4Stakeholder decision and who bears the cost

The three stakeholders want conflicting things. The decision is to change what we show, not what we require, and not to redefine "certified learners". A certified learner is a learner who completed the course and is learning, and that is true. Whether they gained the skill is a different claim, so we add it next to the headline instead of replacing it. No course gets a new completion gate, so completion rate, the certified-learner count and instructor payouts are untouched. The price is paid in marketing language and in partner expectations.

1.5Certificate lifecycle

State is kept separate from level and standard. Merging them would multiply the state machine (Legacy × Outdated × Under Review and so on) for no benefit to engineering.

FieldValuesChanges when
StateActive, Under Review, RevokedEvents below
LevelL0, L1, L2New evidence arrives (moves up). Moves down only through a review outcome
StandardLegacy, V1Never. Fixed at issue or backfill
Version statusCurrent, OutdatedContent ops marks a course revision as material

Transitions

  • Course completion plus evidence evaluation → Active, with level computed.
  • A partner reports a concern. Ops triages it within 1 business day; if accepted, or if ops flags the certificate itself, → Under Review. The partner sees "Under review", the level is hidden, and the learner is told and can respond.
  • Review cleared → Active. Review confirms fraud, after the learner's response window → Revoked (terminal), with the reason recorded.
  • Integrity failures at evaluation (too fast, too many attempts) do not change state. They produce L0 with a flag.
  • Material course revision flips version status to Outdated. State stays Active. The partner sees "Issued against v2, current is v4".

North star: L1+ checks answered "Yes, this was enough to make a decision", per month.
It rises only when assessed supply and partner trust both grow. The answer is optional, so the count is a floor; we also track the response rate.

Is trust failing in practice?

  • Upheld concern and revocation rate on L1+ certificates. It depends on partners reporting, so it is a lagging and incomplete signal.
  • Partner outcome pulse: 90 days after a pilot partner reports hiring or promoting a certificate holder, one question: did they meet expectations? Compared across L0, L1 and L2. This is the only signal that tests whether levels predict real performance.
  • Integrity hold rate: share of quiz passes held at L0 for integrity reasons, by course and by corporate vs self-directed learners. It calibrates the rules rather than measuring failure.
  • L1 from quizzes flagged too easy: share of L1 certificates whose quiz has a pass rate above 95% with a median score above 90%.

1.10Unasked question

Once L1 matters, who keeps quizzes honest? The brief asks how to make certificates trustworthy, but it never asks what happens to quiz quality when the quiz becomes the thing that earns the trust badge and a payout kicker. Instructors control their own quizzes. An easier quiz means more L1 certificates, a higher assessed-learner number for Growth, and a larger kicker for the instructor. Every party benefits from weaker quizzes, and nobody is assigned to notice.

Why it matters. L1 is only as strong as the quiz behind it. If quizzes drift easier, the level quietly loses meaning while the numbers improve. That is exactly the failure the original trust problem describes, moved one step downstream, and it would surface as the same partner complaint six months later.

First action. Before launch, pull score and pass-rate distributions for every quiz course and set a baseline for "quiz looks too easy" (for example, pass rate above 95% with a median score above 90%). Use that baseline to decide whether the kicker applies to all quizzes or only audited ones, and add the share of L1 certificates from flagged quizzes to the metrics.

Concern records have their own status: open (triage) → rejected (certificate stays Active) or accepted (certificate goes Under Review) → cleared or revoked. While a concern is in triage the certificate stays Active and the page shows a "concern raised" banner, with the level still visible.

Not allowed: Active → Revoked directly, any transition without an audit event. Attributes, not states: level moves up on new evidence and moves down only through a review outcome; version status moves Current → Outdated when a revision is marked material; standard (Legacy, V1) never changes.

2.3Level computation rules

  1. Course has no assessment: L0.
  2. Assessment history missing: L0, with the note "history unavailable".
  3. Legacy certificate with a quiz pass but missing timing or attempt records: L0, with the note "records unavailable". New (V1) certificates always have these records, because they are captured at each attempt.
  4. Quiz: L1 only if passed AND time spent is at least the quiz minimum AND attempts within any rolling 72 hours are at most 3. Otherwise L0 with flag "Assessment passed but did not meet integrity criteria".
  5. Proctored or project exam: passed AND identity check on record gives L2. Passed without an identity record gives L1.
  6. Enrollment source never changes the level. Flags never raise a level. No automatic downgrades in the MVP.

Assumption quiz minimum time is set per quiz at 25% of the median historical passing time. Check against attempt data in weeks 1 to 3; adjust the percentage, not the structure.

2.5Migration of existing certificates

  1. Add new columns as nullable. Set every course to version 1 unless version history already exists. Set assessment_type from the course catalog.
  2. Set issued_version to the course version live at the issue date, or 1 if unknown. Generate public_id for every certificate.
  3. Backfill certificate_evidence from historical assessment attempts, identity-check records and exam results. Mark records_complete false where timing or attempt data is missing.
  4. Compute level with the same rules. Set standard to legacy for every certificate issued before the cutover date and state to active.
  5. Dry run on a copy, first in week 6 and again before the real run. Output a report: counts by level, by standard, certificates with missing or partial history, and 100 randomly sampled certificates for manual check by trust ops.
  6. Send learner notices (item 6) to everyone whose Legacy certificate comes out at L0 on an assessed course or with a flag, at least 14 days before the page is visible to partners.
  7. Run for real after Legal and the sample check pass. The job is idempotent and stores rules_version, so it can be re-run safely. Old certificate IDs keep working but need a second check (learner's last initial and issue year), because old IDs may be sequential.

Migration acceptance criteria: 100% of certificates have a level, standard and state. No certificate is given a level above its evidence. The only state transition performed is none → Active. The dry run report matches the real run within 0.1%. No certificate is revoked or removed by the migration. Every learner with a lowered or flagged result has a logged notice.

2.6Features: user stories and acceptance criteria

Item 1. Evidence and level service

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As a partner, I want the level to come from recorded evidence so that I can rely on it more than on a completion flag.
  • As trust ops, I want each level to be explainable from stored evidence so that I can answer a partner's question.
  • All six rules in 2.3 are implemented and unit tested against the 25 records in sample-data.csv, each returning its expected outcome.
  • Levels are precomputed and stored, not calculated at read time.
  • A new assessment attempt or identity-check event triggers a recompute within 5 minutes.
  • Recompute never lowers a level except through a review outcome.

Item 2. Course tagging and versioning

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As content ops, I want to state whether a revision is material when I publish so that old certificates are marked correctly.
  • As a partner, I want to know if a course changed after the certificate was issued so that I can judge how current it is.
  • Every active course has assessment_type and version; publishing is blocked if either is missing.
  • The publish form has a required question "Is this revision material?" and a note field required when yes.
  • Marking material sets version status Outdated on certificates issued against earlier versions within 15 minutes, and never changes their level.
  • A version row cannot be deleted through the app or an admin tool.

Item 3. Legacy backfill

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As the Partnerships lead, I want old certificates labelled honestly so that a partner never confuses them with new ones.
  • As a learner with an older certificate, I want it to stay valid so that my past work still counts.
  • The migration acceptance criteria in 2.5 all pass.
  • A dry run completes on production-size data in under 4 hours.
  • The sample of 100 certificates shows no level above the evidence.
  • No certificate holder loses a certificate; the only change is the displayed level and label.

Item 4. Public certificate check page

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As a partner, I want to enter a certificate ID and see the level and the evidence so that I can decide whether to accept it.
  • As a partner, I want to see context (learner source, course version, Legacy label, flags) so that I read the level correctly.
  • As a learner, I want only my name and the evidence to be public so that my personal data is not exposed.
  • A valid ID returns the result in under 2 seconds at p95.
  • Shows level, evidence list, flags, learner source, version status, Legacy label, a methodology link and the one-click "Was this enough to make a decision?", matching the prototype.
  • Shows learner first name and last initial and the evidence only; no email or other personal data. Not indexed by search engines.
  • Old-format IDs also need the learner's last initial and issue year. An unknown ID returns the same generic message and timing as any other failure; lookups are limited to 30 per minute per IP.
  • Under Review and Revoked states hide level and evidence as specified. A certificate with a concern in triage shows a banner and keeps its level visible.
  • Works on mobile; level is conveyed by text as well as colour (WCAG AA contrast).

Item 5. Concern and review flow

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As a partner, I want to report a certificate that looks wrong so that the platform checks it.
  • As trust ops, I want to triage concerns before anything is hidden so that false reports cannot hide a learner's level.
  • As the trust ops lead, I want revocation to need a reason and my approval so that revocation is defensible.
  • Submitting the form needs a reason and an email address; the concern counts only after the reporter confirms by email link. Limit: 3 concerns per email and 10 per IP per day.
  • The certificate stays Active during triage, and the page shows a "concern raised" banner.
  • Ops sees a queue with age and reason, and must accept or reject within 1 business day (Assumption). Rejecting closes the concern and removes the banner; accepting moves the certificate to Under Review.
  • Clear needs a reason. Revoke needs a reason, the lead role, and a closed or answered learner response window.
  • Every transition writes a certificate_event row. A certificate cannot have two open concerns; later reports attach to the open one.
  • The reporter is emailed the outcome.

Item 6. Learner notice and response

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As a learner whose result is lowered or flagged, I want to be told before it is visible to employers so that I am not surprised.
  • As a learner under review, I want to see the reason and respond so that I can show the evidence is genuine.
  • As trust ops, I want the learner's response attached to the case so that the decision uses both sides.
  • At least 14 days before partners can see the page, every learner named in the migration criteria (2.5, step 6) is emailed their level, the reason, and a contest link.
  • A contest creates a concern of source "learner" in the same queue.
  • When a certificate enters Under Review, the learner is emailed the reason and a response form with a 10 business day window (Assumption).
  • Learner responses are stored on the concern and shown to the reviewer.
  • All notices are logged in learner_notice. The learner can open the public check page for their own certificate.

Item 7. Methodology page and privacy review

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As Legal, I want every level to have written, checkable criteria so that the word "Verified" is defensible.
  • As a partner, I want to know what each level does and does not prove so that I do not over-read it.
  • States each level's criteria, what each level does not prove (for example, L1 does not verify identity), the Legacy approach, the concern process and the learner response right.
  • Signed off by Legal before the pilot, together with an updated privacy notice and terms that tell learners the certificate ID can be checked publicly. Versioned, with the version shown on the page.
  • The word "Verified" appears only in connection with L2.

Item 8. Learner upgrade flow (stretch, built last, cut first)

User storiesAcceptance criteria
  • As a learner with an L0 or L1 certificate, I want to add a higher-level assessment so that my certificate can show stronger evidence.
  • Starts only if items 1 to 7 are accepted by the end of week 10.
  • A learner with an L0 or L1 certificate sees "Add an exam" when a higher-level assessment exists for the course.
  • Passing it adds an evidence row and raises the level on the same certificate ID. It can never lower it.

2.7Non-functional requirements and open questions

  • Access control: ops screens limited to the trust ops and lead roles. All changes are audited.
  • Privacy: the certificate ID acts as the key to a public page showing name, course and level. Legal reviews this under item 7. Check feedback stores no user identifier.
  • Security: rate limiting, no sequential IDs, no personal data in URLs, abuse monitoring on the concern form.
  • Open: are identity checks currently stored in a queryable form? (Blocks L2 correctness; check in weeks 1 to 3.)
  • Open: do historical quiz records include timestamps and attempt counts? (Decides how many Legacy certificates stay at L0.)
  • Open: can course versions be reconstructed from the CMS history, or is every course version 1? (Affects the Outdated notice for old revisions.)
  • Open: does the catalog already record assessment type for every course? If not, content ops must tag them.
  • Open: who owns the "material revision" decision in content ops? Needs a named role before launch.
  • Open: which email service sends learner and reporter notices, and does it support the volume of the pre-launch notice?

Decision rule: results are reviewed at the end of week 3. Any one row triggering is enough to revise the design before sprint planning; two or more means the PRD is reopened.

4.4Senior bonus: the underperforming promotions

A partner promoted people based on "trusted" certificates and several underperformed. Before changing anything, find out which of three things happened. They need different fixes and the evidence separating them is already in our data.

HypothesisHow to tellWhat I would change
Mismatch in expectationsGet the list of promoted people and their certificate IDs. Do not defend the model before the data is read. A fair caveat to raise carefully: promotion outcomes depend on many things beyond a course certificate, so the question is whether certified people do better than comparable uncertified people, not whether every certified person performed.

Process

5.1AI usage note

Where AI helped. I used Claude as a drafting and building partner, and worked through each section in chat before it went into the document. It proposed options for the trust model, lifecycle and MVP cutline for me to choose from. It drafted the cost estimate, metrics, edge case register, PRD and discovery plan, and built the HTML document, the prototype and the three diagrams. It wrote a script that ran all 25 sample records through the prototype's level logic and compared them with the expected outcomes. It also reviewed its own draft and found contradictions I had not noticed, such as an integrity metric that could never be non-zero and a north star we could not measure. Finally, it measured each deliverable against its page limit by printing it. The decisions are mine; the numbers are placeholder assumptions that I reviewed and accepted.

One suggestion I rejected. The AI proposed that the headline in pitches move from "certified learners" to "assessed learners (L1+)", which would have cut the headline by about 55%. I rejected it. "Certified" means the learner completed the course and is learning, which is true, and redefining it would punish Growth for something that is not wrong. Whether the learner gained the skill is a different claim. So the document keeps "certified learners" unchanged and adds "assessed learners" as a second number beside it.
